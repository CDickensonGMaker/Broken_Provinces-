## Living World v1 — schedules and presence (8/2)

Caleb's directive: *"the NPCs should be living inside this world."* v1 is
schedules and presence, built to `docs/design/living_world_scout.md`, which
distils RECONgame's proven simulation architecture. Seven commits.

**The clock is the one GameManager already had.** It owned `game_time`,
`time_scale` and `current_day`; it lacked a way to hang work off a (day, hour)
and one door for time skips. Both added. `advance()` is that door and
`advance_time()` delegates to it, so RestManager, WaitUI, jail, rest spots,
fast travel and the sea voyage all route through it without touching their
call sites. `hour_advanced` fires once per whole hour crossed, in order — an
eight-hour rest replays eight hours rather than jumping them. The per-entry
dedup key is ported verbatim from RECON, where a kind-wide key silently dropped
all but the first of three same-hour events. The clock is now saved, which
RECON never did (format 9 → 10).

**Two save bugs fell out of writing it.** `active_goblin_camps` was rolled once
at new game and persisted nowhere, so every loaded save had zero camps active
and `is_goblin_camp_active` answered false for all three. And GameManager was
in no serialization guard at all; it is now, with a written reason for each of
the fourteen fields it deliberately does not save.

**The scope was decided by a measurement, not a plan.** Station positions were
read by booting each town headless rather than scraped from the spawner, because
several NPCs are moved after the level script names a spot. Booting each town a
*second* time and diffing found that 76 of the 189 town NPCs get a different
`npc_id` **and** a different position every boot — they are the ambient
population, named out of a pool and placed by `randf()`. An authored record
keyed on one of those ids is dead data. Nobody had noticed because
`validate_content` scrapes ids out of source text, where generated ones never
appear. So 112 authored NPCs get records and the ambient 76 get a runtime
record from the archetype their spawner names. Same machinery, no dead ids.

**Twenty-two archetypes**, one per trade the towns actually contain. The trades
were not invented — every one was already written into a display name or an id.
Archetype hours are authored so no quest-holding trade is behind a door between
09:00 and 17:00, which makes the no-soft-lock guarantee hold by construction
rather than by 112 separate strokes of luck.

**Presence is the gate and there is no second boolean.** `ShopUI.open_for()` is
the single door every shop path funnels through, so that is where the test
lives. Elder Moor's and Dalhurst's `_update_civilian_visibility()` — which
blinked a whole town out of existence at 21:00 and back at dawn, everyone still
standing exactly where they had been — are deleted.

**Ship gate, measured:** Elder Moor holds **3 people at 03:00 and 33 at 13:00**,
and the shopkeeper's shop refuses `ShopUI.open_for` at 03:00 and takes it at
13:00 — tested through the real door, not a mirrored flag.

`tools/check_living_world.tscn` is the thirteenth gate, 33 checks. Two rules
sit there rather than in `validate.ps1` because a source-text validator would
lie about them: "every scheduled npc_id spawns somewhere" (twenty ids are
assigned in ways no regex sees) and the roster diff. The first earned its keep
immediately — `colm_thornfield` was a pooled name that came up twice in the
stability census by luck and was not there on the third boot.

**Deferred to v2/v3, per the scout:** visible walking (`_bt_settle`), claimed
tavern seats, households, and reactive overrides. v1 transitions are teleports.

**Outstanding, and all his:** five rulings in
`docs/audits/wave_b_dispositions.md` (LW-1 to LW-5), the largest being whether
the ambient population should be seeded off `world_seed` so a town's people are
the same people every time you come back. And the eye gate: **nothing here has
been watched.** Three at 03:00 and thirty-three at 13:00 is the right shape; whether
an empty night reads as atmospheric or just empty is a thing only he can say.
