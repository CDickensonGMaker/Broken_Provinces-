# The EverQuest Technique — verified, then turned into our pipeline rules

*2026-08-02, Wyrm. Companion to `docs/design/ART_GUIDE.md` (which names EverQuest 1999 as
the visual north star) and `docs/design/citizen_review/REVIEW.md` (which records what the
four citizen masters actually are). This document is **research + a work list**. Nothing in
section 3 has been executed; the Blender changes are a follow-up.*

Caleb's instinct, in his words: *"it's more about good textures and then shapes that help
build the form."* The research says that is right, and it is more specific than he stated:
**EQ's geometry exists to make a silhouette, and everything else — the entire wardrobe of
the game, every armour tier, every rank of NPC — is a texture index and a tint on a body
mesh that never changes.**

---

## 1. THE VERIFIED FACTS

### 1.1 Texture resolution — smaller than anyone remembers

| Era | Resolution | Source |
|---|---|---|
| EQ launch, 1999 | **32×32** character textures; some parts 32×64 | [P99 Visual Enhancements](https://wiki.project1999.com/Visual_Enhancements) |
| Shadows of Luclin, 2001 | 256×256, a few 512×512 | same |

A 1999 EQ human's chest, arm and leg textures were each roughly a **32-pixel square**. Every
belt, buckle, stud, seam and fold on a classic EQ character was painted at that size. This
is the single most calibrating fact in the document: our 32×48 face rect is not stingy by EQ
standards, it is *typical*, and our per-garment texture budget can be small and still beat
the reference.

### 1.2 Geometry budget

| Model | Tris / verts | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Classic EQ player character | ~700 polys (community recollection) | [Daz3D forum: Game Art Polygon Counts](https://www.daz3d.com/forums/discussion/14825/game-art-polygon-counts) |
| Classic EQ end-game boss | ~1,000 polys | same |
| EverQuest Online Adventures (PS2, same house) | 400–2,000, **~800 typical** | [EQOA Revival wiki](https://wiki.eqoarevival.com/index.php/Graphic_Model_Extraction) |
| *EverQuest Legends* recreation of the look | up to **1,250** verts/faces/tris | [80.lv interview](https://80.lv/articles/interview-how-everquest-legends-recreates-classic-everquest-s-iconic-visual-style) |

The 700/1,000 figures are forum recollection, not a shipped spec sheet — treat them as the
right order of magnitude, corroborated by EQOA's measured 400–2,000 range from the same
studio. Our masters dress out at **606–698 tris** (REVIEW.md), which lands exactly on the
classic-player number. **The budget is not the problem. The texture is.**

The 80.lv interview also names where the charm actually lives: *"a personality and charm
that cannot be replicated, and a lot more detail than they sometimes get credit for"* —
attributed to art direction and silhouette, with terrain described as *"extremely harsh
terrain mesh… N64 graphics. Very rough and edgy."* Hard edges, not smooth form.

### 1.3 The reuse machine — the part worth stealing

Classic EQ shipped, per race and gender, a fixed inventory of **texture sets** and
**head/face/hair/beard** variants against **one body mesh**. Shendare's EQ Race Inventory,
generated by parsing the client's `.s3d`/`.wld` files, lists them
([EQRI](http://www.shendare.com/EQ/Emu/EQRI/), [Titanium
races](http://shendare.com/eq/emu/eqri/Titanium_EQRaces.htm)):

| Race | Textures | Heads | Faces | Hair | Beards | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Human Male (race 1, gender 0) | **00–04, 10–16** (12) | 00–03 | 00–07 | 00–03 | 00–05 | — |
| Human Female | 00–04, 10–16 (12) | 00–03 | 00–07 | 00–03 | — | — |
| Barbarian Male (race 2) | 00–03 (4) | 00–03 | 00–07 | 00–03 | 00–05 | Tattoo 00–08 |
| Barbarian Female | 00–03 (4) | 00–03 | 00–07 | 00–03 | — | Tattoo 00–08 |
| Wood Elf Male | 00–03 (4) | 00–03 | 00–07 | 00–03 | — | — |

**Do the arithmetic on the human male.** One body mesh × 12 texture sets × 4 heads × 8 faces
× 4 hairs × 6 beards = **9,216 visually distinct humans**. Ignore the head entirely — at the
distance you actually see a townsman, it is **12 texture sets × nothing else = 12 people, from
one mesh, with zero extra polygons.**

That is the trick, stated as a ratio: **EQ got ~12× read-at-distance variety per body mesh
out of texture alone, and ~9,000× total variety out of texture plus four small head parts.**

### 1.4 What those texture numbers *mean* — armour is an index, not a model

Material IDs in the classic client
([P99 fashion thread](https://www.project1999.com/forums/archive/index.php/t-275294.html)):

```
0 = cloth      1 = leather      2 = chain      3 = plate      4 = monk leather
10–16          = robes (the seven robe looks, human/erudite/elf casters)
17–23          = Velious armour, appended as expansions shipped
```

There is no plate *model*. There is a plate *texture* on the same naked body mesh. An
EQEmulator veteran states it plainly: in the Luclin engine *"there are only 3 actual looks
per race: leather, chain and plate, plus robes, and in the special case of monks/bst there's
also a Gi"* ([EQEmulator forums](https://www.eqemulator.org/forums/archive/index.php/t-25664.html)).
Kunark chain reused the chain texture on old-world races and borrowed the fourth slot on
Iksar, *because Iksar had no proper plate texture* — the tiers are texture slots, and when a
slot is missing the art is re-pointed, never re-modelled.

### 1.5 The variation is per-SLOT and tinted

EQEmu's `npc_types` exposes the same knobs the client always had: a global `texture`, plus
per-slot `armstexture`, `legstexture`, `bracertexture`, `feettexture`, `handtexture`, plus a
separate `helmtexture` for the head, plus an `npc_types_tint` table carrying per-slot RGB
([EQEmulator forums, "Question about armor textures"](https://www.eqemulator.org/forums/showthread.php?t=42428)).

So a classic EQ NPC's whole appearance is: **body mesh + head index + 6 texture indices + 6
tint colours.** Every guard in a city is the same 700 triangles with different numbers.

### 1.6 The file layer, for the record

The client stores characters in `.s3d` archives containing `.wld` scene files. A `.wld` is a
header, a string hash, and an array of fragments; **0x31** is a *material list* and **0x30**
is a *material* pointing at a **0x05** texture-bitmap reference
([OpenEQ WLD wiki](https://github.com/daeken/OpenEQ/wiki/WLD)). Alternate skins are
alternate *material lists* against the same mesh — the geometry fragment is not duplicated.
`LanternExtractor` parses the 1999–2001 client and exports "textured meshes, skeletal
animations, **skins**" as separate concerns, and equipment as its own meshes+animations
([LanternExtractor](https://github.com/LanternEQ/LanternExtractor)). Texture bitmaps are
named per model, e.g. `FEMCH0101.bmp`, `FEMLG0101.bmp` — *model code + body part + set
number + frame* ([WLD Editor Suite discussion](https://www.eqemulator.org/forums/archive/index.php/t-38210.html)).

Read the naming and the whole system falls out: **`CH` chest, `LG` legs, `HE` head, then a
two-digit set number, then a two-digit variant.** Armour tiers are the set number.

### 1.7 Why EQ NPCs read at distance

No source states this as a rule, so this is inference from the facts above plus the shipped
art, and it is stated as inference:

- **Value blocking, not detail.** At 32×32 a texture cannot carry detail, so EQ art is 2–4
  large flat value zones per body part with hard boundaries. That is what survives to 12
  screen pixels.
- **Silhouette is decided by very few shapes** — a hood, a robe hem, a helmet, a shoulder
  line. Everything internal to the outline is paint.
- **Painted lighting.** No normal maps existed. Folds, shadow under the belt, the light on the
  top of a shoulder — all painted into the texel and *baked to a fixed light direction from
  above*. This is why EQ characters look lit even under flat vertex lighting.
- **Hue separates the crowd; value separates the man from the background.**

## 2. THE TECHNIQUE, AS RULES FOR OUR PIPELINE

### The finding that matters most

**Right now every citizen in Broken Provinces wears the same four colours.**
`scripts/characters/visuals/citizen_dresser.gd` binds the garb palette to `_shared_garb`, one
`BaseMaterial3D` for the entire game, deliberately ("neither carries per-instance state in
v1"). The face atlas slides per instance; the clothes do not. Our variation is therefore
**2 vests × 3 sleeves × ≤2 legs × 2 hood × 2 apron = 24 garment silhouettes in one colourway**,
against EQ's 12 colourways per body before you even count geometry.

We are currently doing the *opposite* of the EQ technique: all our variety is in mesh
toggles, none in texture. Caleb's instinct is a correction, and the numbers back it.

### The five rules

**RULE 1 — Colour is the variation; geometry is the silhouette.**
Two citizens must differ in palette before they differ in polygons. A new NPC "look" costs
one texture page and zero triangles. If a proposed variant does not change the outline, it
is not allowed to be a mesh.

**RULE 2 — One mesh, many pages.** A garment variant is a `uv1_offset` into a garb atlas,
exactly as a face is. The mesh that carries a merchant's apron-front, a priest's cassock, a
guard's studded jerkin and a beggar's rags is **the same `garb_vest`** at three different
atlas offsets and three different tints.

**RULE 3 — Mesh toggle only where the OUTLINE changes.** The test is: *does it break the
silhouette against the sky?* Then it is geometry. Otherwise it is paint.

| Reads in TEXTURE (delete or never build the mesh) | Needs MESH (silhouette breaks) |
|---|---|
| vest vs. tunic vs. cassock vs. rags | hood up (changes the head outline) |
| laces, buttons, belts, buckles, pouches | skirt vs. trousers (leg outline) |
| apron *front*, coin belt, sash, tabard | sleeve length (the cuff is an outline edge) |
| armour plates, mail, studs, trim, embroidery | hats and helmets (socketed props) |
| boot tops, glove cuffs, rolled sleeves *on a sleeved arm* | shoulder pauldrons, capes, backpacks |
| dirt, wear, blood, rank insignia | the apron's hem overlap (14 tris — cheap, keep it) |

**RULE 4 — Paint the light, because nothing else will.** No normal maps (ART_GUIDE spec).
Every fold, every shadow under a belt, every highlight on a shoulder is painted, with **one
fixed light direction: from above and slightly front-left.** Consistency of that direction
across every page is what makes a crowd look like one game instead of a kitbash.

**RULE 5 — The 12-pixel test.** Before a page is accepted, view it at ~12 px tall (the size a
townsman actually occupies across a market square). If the citizen is not identifiable as
*a silhouette plus two colour blocks*, the page has failed and the fix is bigger blocks and
more value contrast — never more detail.

**Corollary — tint multiplies everything.** EQ carried per-slot RGB tint on top of the
texture index (§1.5). Godot gives us the same for free via `albedo_color` on a per-instance
material. Paint every page in **neutral mid-value greys and desaturated browns** so a tint
multiply lands where intended. `N` painted pages × `M` tints = `N×M` looks for the cost of
`N` paintings. This is where the 12× becomes 100×.

## 3. THE WORK LIST — masters, atlas, dresser

*Not executed. This is the follow-up brief.*

### 3.A The choice to make first

**Option A — the garb atlas (recommended now).** Keep the body's flat skin patch. Re-UV the
*garb meshes* into a paged atlas and slide them per instance. One build run of stages
03→09, no change to the body, no change to the face contract.

**Option B — the full EQ way (wave 2).** Give the *body itself* real UVs — torso front/back,
arms, legs, feet — into a body atlas, so clothing can be painted onto skin the way EQ did,
and sleeve length / boot tops / gloves become texture instead of geometry.

**Blocking fact for Option B, found in the code:** the body currently samples a **32×16 flat
skin patch** (`citizen_common.py`, `FACE_ROWS = 48`, `SKIN_ROWS = 16`) — one solid tone, no
detail, by design. *The EQ trick of painting clothes onto the body is physically impossible
on the masters as they stand.* That is why every garment today has to be a mesh. Option B is
the structural fix; Option A gets 80% of the win without touching the atlas contract that
`bake_us_faces.py` compatibility depends on.

**Recommendation: do A now, schedule B for the armour/undead waves**, where painted mail and
plate on a bare body is the whole point.

**If B is ever done, one law comes with it:** the body page and the face cell must be
**tone-locked by construction** — body page *k* and face column *k* painted from the same
skin swatch, and the dresser derives the face column from the body page rather than rolling
it separately. Otherwise this is the 2026-07-29 black-head bug wearing a new coat, and
dresser law 3 exists precisely because that bug is expensive.

### 3.B `tools/citizens/03_make_textures.py` — the garb atlas contract

Replace the 64×64 four-zone placeholder with a **paged atlas**:

```
512 x 512 PNG        4 columns x 4 rows  =  16 GARB PAGES
page                 128 x 128 px
  within a page, four zones of 64 x 64:
    (0,0)   VEST      the torso garment, front and back
    (64,0)  PANTS     trousers AND skirt sample this zone
    (0,64)  SLEEVES   both arms
    (64,64) EXTRA     apron and hood
page stride (uv1_offset) = (0.25, 0.25)
```

- 16 pages ≈ EQ's 12 texture sets per race. Sized to match the reference deliberately.
- 64×64 per garment zone is **twice** EQ's 32×32 chest texture. Generous, on purpose, because
  our garb zone must serve where EQ had a separate chest/arms/legs file each.
- **Resolution-independent, like the face atlas.** 1024² gives 128 px zones with no UV change,
  as long as it stays 4×4 pages of four quadrants.
- Keep generating a *placeholder* version of this file so the build never depends on
  Caleb having painted yet — same discipline as the face atlas.

### 3.C `tools/citizens/05_build_garb.py`

1. **Re-point `ZONE` into page 0.** Every rect divides by 4 and gains the inset that keeps
   nearest-neighbour sampling off the seam:
   ```python
   ZONE = {"vest":    (0.005, 0.130, 0.120, 0.245),
           "pants":   (0.130, 0.130, 0.245, 0.245),
           "sleeves": (0.005, 0.005, 0.120, 0.120),
           "extra":   (0.130, 0.005, 0.245, 0.120)}
   ```
   (numbers to be re-derived against the final zone inset — the shape is what is being
   specified, not the sixth decimal.)
2. **Delete `garb_vest_laced`.** Its entire content is a 4-quad strip down the chest — the
   textbook Rule-3 violation. Saves 68 tris and one mesh per master; the laced look becomes
   a painted page. `build_vest(laced=True)` goes with it.
3. **Keep** `garb_apron` (14 tris, real hem overlap), `garb_hood`, `garb_skirt`,
   `garb_pants`, and all three sleeve lengths — every one of them changes the outline.
4. **Consider adding one mesh, not removing another:** a `garb_robe` — a single skirted tube
   from shoulder to ankle. It is the one silhouette we cannot fake (priests of three gods,
   cultists, mages, the whole Wave-5 lich/wight family), and EQ gave robes their own seven
   texture slots for exactly this reason. Budget ~90 tris; the dressed total stays under 700
   because a robe *replaces* vest+pants+skirt.
5. **Re-assert budgets** after the changes: garb total drops by 68×4 = 272 tris before the
   robe is added.

### 3.D `tools/citizens/08_export_glb.py` / `09_validate_glb.py`

- Assert the garb material's UVs all fall inside page 0 (`u,v < 0.25`). A garment whose UVs
  straddle a page boundary will smear two outfits together the moment the dresser slides it,
  and it will look like a texture bug rather than a UV bug.
- Assert `garb_vest_laced` is *gone*, so it cannot come back through a stale stage file.

### 3.E `scripts/characters/visuals/citizen_dresser.gd` — the real behavioural change

1. **`_shared_garb` must die.** Duplicate the garb material per instance, exactly as the face
   material is duplicated, for exactly the same reason (law 2 — "a fireteam of octuplets").
   The comment saying garb "carries no per-instance state in v1" is the thing being
   retired.
2. **Add `GARB_COLS = 4`, `GARB_ROWS = 4`, `GARB_STRIDE = Vector2(0.25, 0.25)`** and a
   `garb_page_offset(index)` mirroring `face_offset()`. Public, so the gate can compute the
   expectation without duplicating the arithmetic.
3. **Roll the page from the same seeded RNG** and return it in the loadout dictionary as
   `out["garb_page"]` / `out["garb_offset"]` — the dresser's contract is that it reports what
   was *actually applied*.
4. **Archetype drives the page, seed drives the tint.** A smith's page is not random; his
   *colour* is. Extend the existing `APRON_ARCHETYPES` idea into a
   `ARCHETYPE_PAGE: Dictionary` (`"priest_chronos" → 8`, `"guard" → 4`, …) with a seeded
   fallback across the "commoner" page range for anyone unlisted. This is EQEmu's
   `npc_types.texture` field, restated in GDScript.
5. **Per-instance tint.** `mine.albedo_color = tint` from a small authored palette
   (12–16 period-plausible dye colours), seeded per npc_id. This is `npc_types_tint`.
6. **Split garb into upper and lower materials — evaluate, do not assume.** EQ tinted *per
   slot*; one material means a citizen's shirt and trousers always tint together, which is
   the least convincing thing a crowd can do. The cost is one extra draw call per citizen
   and one extra material duplication. **Measure it before adopting it**; the tri-budget
   memory law applies (tri cuts moved FPS ~0 in RECON; draw calls are the thing that
   matters), so this one deserves a number, not a preference.
7. **Warn once when a model has no garb atlas bound** — the capability-degradation law. A
   citizen silently wearing page 0 forever is the fifteen-unused-helmets bug again.

- Add a variant-wall render to `07_render_review.py` showing **one master × 16 pages × 3

## 4. PAINTING GUIDANCE FOR CALEB

### 4.1 Resolution and file

| Asset | Paint at | Ships at | Grid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Face atlas | **512×512** | 512×512 | 8 cols × 4 rows, cell 64×96 face + 64×32 skin |
| Garb atlas | **512×512** | 512×512 | 4 × 4 pages of 128 px, four 64 px zones per page |
| Hair palette | 64×64 | 64×64 | 4 colour strips |

Paint at ship resolution, not above it. Downsampling a 2K painting to 512 produces mush —
the boundaries blur, and boundaries are the entire read at this scale. **Nearest-neighbour
everything; no anti-aliasing, no soft brushes, no layer blur.** Hard-edged pencil at 100%
opacity is the correct tool, and it is the tool EQ's artists had.

### 4.2 What goes where, per garb page

Each 64×64 zone is one garment part. Within the VEST zone, plan on:

```
 upper 40%   shoulders and chest — the lightest values on the page (top light)
 middle 35%  the body of the garment, the largest flat block, one hue
 lower 25%   the hem and belt line — the darkest band, hard edge
```

That is the whole recipe: **light on top, mid in the middle, dark at the hem, hard edges
between.** It reads as a lit cylinder at 12 pixels and it is exactly what a 32×32 EQ chest
texture does.

### 4.3 The shading conventions (there are no normal maps)

1. **One light, always: from above, tilted slightly to the front-left.** Never re-decide this
   per page. A single inconsistent page makes the crowd look wrong and nobody can say why.
2. **Three values per material, four at most.** Highlight / base / shadow / (accent). More
   values do not survive to 12 pixels; they just desaturate the block.
3. **Paint the occlusion, not the object.** The dark line under a belt, in an armpit, where a
   sleeve meets a shoulder, under a hood's brim — those contact shadows are what makes flat
   geometry read as form. They are worth more than the belt itself.
4. **Folds are lines, not gradients.** A fold is one dark pixel-line with one light
   pixel-line beside it. At this scale a soft fold is invisible.
5. **Keep it desaturated and mid-value so tint multiply works.** A page painted saturated
   crimson can only ever be crimson or a darker crimson. A page painted in warm greys becomes
   sixteen dyes.
6. **Value separates the man from the background; hue separates men from each other.** Our
   towns are earth and stone — so citizens should sit *brighter* than the ground and differ
   from each other in hue, not in brightness.
7. **The seam rule.** Leave 2 px of the correct colour inside every zone boundary. Nearest
   filtering plus a UV a fraction off will otherwise sample the neighbouring garment, and it
   will look like a shader bug for a week.

### 4.4 Face rect specifics (unchanged contract, restated)

From REVIEW.md, still binding: the face rect is the **top three-quarters** of a cell; the
bottom quarter is a **flat swatch of the tone the neck ends on** — the entire body samples one
pixel of it, so if it does not match the jaw, the head floats. The leftmost and rightmost
eighth of the face rect is the **back of the head** — scalp and hair there, never features.

### 4.5 What to paint FIRST

In order, and the order is chosen so that each step is visible in-game before the next
begins:

1. **Face atlas, row 0, all 8 skin-tone columns — 8 faces.** This is the only atlas whose
   contract is already final and already bound in the dresser (`FACE_ATLAS_PATH`). Eight
   painted faces immediately replace the procedural placeholder for *every citizen in the
   game*, because the dresser already rolls across all 32 cells and the remaining rows can
   stay placeholder while he works. Highest-leverage 8 drawings in the project.
2. **Garb page 0 — "commoner", all four zones.** The moment 3.B/3.C land. One page, painted
   in warm neutral greys, proves the tint system: page 0 × 12 dyes is already a more varied
   crowd than the game has today.
3. **Face atlas rows 1–3** — 24 more faces, filling out the population.
4. **Garb pages 1–3: labourer, merchant, guard.** The three archetypes with the most bodies
   in the world (ART_GUIDE Wave 1).
5. **Garb pages 8–10: the three priesthoods** — Chronos grey, Gaela green, Morthane black.
   These are the pages that most want the robe mesh from 3.C.4, and they are the clearest
   case in the game for EQ's robe slots 10–16.

Everything after that is filling the atlas, and filling the atlas is what an EQ artist did
for a living.
